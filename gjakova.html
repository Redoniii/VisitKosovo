City of the old bazaar

Discover Gjakova

Gjakova (Gjakovë) lies in south-western Kosovo, close to the border with Albania. The city grew up in Ottoman times around one of the largest and oldest bazaars in the region, where craftsmen and traders worked side by side for centuries.

Much of the old bazaar burned during the war in 1999 and has since been carefully rebuilt. Today its cobbled streets are full of cafés, restaurants and small shops, and it is one of the most atmospheric places in Kosovo to spend an evening.

1 Grand Bazaar

Centuries of trade under one roofline.

The Grand Bazaar (Çarshia e Madhe) is a long, winding street of low wooden shops dating back to the Ottoman era. Each part of the bazaar was once home to a different craft, from tailors and silversmiths to saddle-makers.

Rebuilt after the war, it is once again the lively centre of the city — the place to walk, eat and shop.

2 Hadum Mosque

Painted walls from the 16th century.

The Hadum Mosque, built at the end of the 16th century, stands at the edge of the Grand Bazaar and is one of the oldest mosques in Gjakova. It was the centre around which the bazaar first grew.

It is best known for its richly painted interior, with colourful decoration covering the walls and dome.

3 Terzi Bridge

The Tailors' Bridge.

Near the village of Bishtazhin, just outside the city, the Terzi Bridge (Ura e Terzive) crosses the Erenik river with a long row of stone arches. It probably dates from the end of the 15th century and was rebuilt in the 18th; it takes its name from the tailors' guild of Gjakova.

It is one of the longest old stone bridges in Kosovo and a peaceful spot for a walk by the river.
