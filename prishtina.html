The capital of Kosovo

Discover the beauty of Prishtina

Prishtina is a vibrant and dynamic city, with a rich cultural heritage, charming architecture, and a thriving arts and food scene. From its bustling markets and street vendors, to its traditional teahouses and modern cafes, Prishtina is a city full of surprises and wonders waiting to be explored.

Whether you're interested in history, culture, or just soaking up the local atmosphere, Prishtina has something for everyone, and is sure to leave a lasting impression on all who visit.

1 Ethnographic Museum

Step inside an Ottoman-era home.

The museum is housed in an Ottoman-era mansion and contains exhibits showcasing the traditional culture and history of Kosovo's diverse ethnic groups. It is considered one of the finest cultural institutions in the Balkans and is known for its well-curated exhibits, including traditional clothing, household objects, and weapons.

The Ethnographic Museum is also a popular tourist destination, attracting visitors from around the world who are interested in learning about the rich cultural heritage of Kosovo.

2 Imperial Mosque & Clock Tower

The heart of the old town.

The Imperial Mosque, also known as the Sultan Mehmet Fatih Mosque or Xhamia e Mbretit, was built in 1461 on the orders of the Ottoman sultan Mehmed II. It is one of the oldest buildings in Prishtina, and its large dome and tall minaret still dominate the old town.

Right next to it stands the 19th-century Clock Tower (Sahat Kulla). Together with the Great Hamam and the old bazaar streets around them, they make up the historic core of the city — an easy walk from the Ethnographic Museum.

3 Newborn Monument

The symbol of independence.

The Newborn Monument in Prishtina is a large sculpture located in the city center, erected to commemorate the declaration of independence from Serbia in 2008. The monument spells out the word “Newborn” in giant letters.

It has become a symbol of Kosovo's independence and a popular tourist attraction, drawing visitors from around the world to take photos with it.

4 Mother Teresa Cathedral

The city's newest landmark.

The Cathedral of Saint Mother Teresa is the Catholic cathedral of Prishtina, dedicated to the Albanian-born saint. Plans for the cathedral were approved in 2007, and it was consecrated in 2017.

Its tall bell tower is one of the most recognisable sights on the skyline, and visitors can go up the tower for a view over the whole city. It sits a short walk from Mother Teresa Boulevard, Prishtina's lively pedestrian main street, lined with cafés and shops.

5 National Library of Kosovo

The country's largest library.

The National Library of Kosovo is the largest library in the country. Located in the capital city of Prishtina, it serves as a cultural and educational center, providing access to books, magazines, and other resources to the people of Kosovo.

The library has a rich collection of over 500,000 items, including rare books and manuscripts, and it is an important resource for researchers, students, and others who are interested in Kosovo's history and cultural heritage.

6 Germia Park

The city's green escape.

Germia is a large regional park of forested hills on the north-eastern edge of Prishtina, only minutes from the centre. Its walking and cycling trails are popular with locals all year round.

In summer, families come for picnics and for the park's big outdoor swimming pool; in winter, the snowy forest makes for quiet walks.

Bear Sanctuary Prishtina

A forested sanctuary near Lake Badovc that gives a safe home to brown bears rescued from captivity. Visitors can walk the paths and watch the bears in large natural enclosures.

Good to know

  • Getting therePrishtina International Airport “Adem Jashari” is about 15 km from the city centre.
  • CurrencyKosovo uses the euro (€).
  • LanguageAlbanian and Serbian are official; many young people also speak English.
  • Getting aroundThe centre is compact and easy to explore on foot; taxis are cheap for longer trips.
  • Don't missA macchiato in one of the cafés on Mother Teresa Boulevard — café culture is a big part of city life.
